An almanac of the estuaryLow Water

Four maps of the Holm estuary at the bottom of the tide, each pulled on a duplicator one drum at a time. Blue for the silt, black for the banks, a fluorescent green dot wherever someone stood and looked. Nothing on these sheets is a fill colour: every colour is an ink that went through a stencil, landed a little off, and mixed with the one under it.

Sheets
I–IV, A3, 60 copies of each
Drums
aqua, black, fluorescent green, green, bright red, yellow, light grey, brown
Paper
uncoated white 90 g and natural 100 g
Showing
10–26 October 2026, the Tide Shed, Holmsey

Drop a photograph on any sheet and it goes through that sheet's drums: its darks become ink.

The other three

Each sheet takes a different view of the same water: from above, from the bank, as a chart, and as the weather over it. Each keeps to the inks it was given. Where two inks cross you get a third colour, which is the only way a riso makes one.

Sheet I, drum by drum

The first sheet pulled again, one stencil at a time. Every drum starts as a master drawn in black. The press screens it into grain, lands it a fraction of a millimetre off, and lays it over whatever is already on the paper. That is why the dots never sit quite on the banks. Pull sheet I again and these follow it.
